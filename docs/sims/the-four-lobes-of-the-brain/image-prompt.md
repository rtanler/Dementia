Critical Rule

This image must contain absolutely no text, labels, arrows, callout lines, numbers, or annotation marks of any kind. All labeling will be added as an interactive HTML overlay from a separate data file. Any embedded text will conflict with the overlay system and must not appear.

Image Specifications

Format: PNG
Dimensions: 1200 × 900 px (landscape, 4:3 ratio)
Background: Clean white (#FFFFFF)
Style: Scientific watercolor illustration with soft textures and gentle shading; suitable for a high school / adult patient-education textbook on dementia. Hand-painted watercolor feel with clear anatomical accuracy.

What to Draw

A lateral (side) view of a human brain showing the left cerebral hemisphere as it would appear from the left side. The brain should be oriented with the frontal lobe on the LEFT side of the canvas and the occipital lobe on the RIGHT side of the canvas. The brain should fill approximately 80-85% of the canvas, horizontally centered. Leave a 5-8% margin on all sides for overlay markers that may sit outside the brain boundary.

The image should show:

The recognizable outline of the human cerebrum (left hemisphere, lateral view).
Characteristic gyri and sulci (the wrinkled ridges and grooves of the cortex) painted with soft watercolor texture.
The four cerebral lobes distinctly separated by subtle but visible boundary lines along the major sulci (central sulcus between frontal and parietal, lateral/Sylvian fissure above the temporal lobe, and a soft boundary for the occipital lobe).
A small visible portion of the cerebellum below and behind (painted in a neutral soft gray or tan so it does not compete with the four lobes).
A small visible portion of the brainstem descending below the cerebrum (also neutral gray/tan).

Color-Coded Lobes

Each lobe should be painted in a distinct soft watercolor wash so that students can easily distinguish the four regions. Colors should be saturated enough to read clearly but soft enough to still show the gyri and sulci texture underneath.

1. Frontal Lobe (BLUE)

Position: Front third of the brain, on the LEFT side of the canvas, behind where the forehead would be. Extends from the front tip of the brain back to the central sulcus.
Visual: Soft sky blue wash (#6FA8DC), watercolor style. Covers roughly the front 35% of the cerebrum. Should show the characteristic rounded front edge and gentle gyri texture.

2. Parietal Lobe (GREEN)

Position: Upper middle of the brain, directly behind the frontal lobe and above the temporal lobe. Sits on top of the brain toward the back.
Visual: Soft sage green wash (#7FBF7F), watercolor style. Roughly wedge-shaped, occupying the upper-middle portion of the cerebrum.

3. Temporal Lobe (YELLOW)

Position: Lower side of the brain, below the lateral (Sylvian) fissure, in the region where the ears would be. Sits below the frontal and parietal lobes.
Visual: Soft golden yellow wash (#F1C232), watercolor style. Forms the lower lateral bulge of the cerebrum with a rounded bottom edge.

4. Occipital Lobe (PURPLE)

Position: Back of the brain, on the RIGHT side of the canvas. The rearmost portion of the cerebrum.
Visual: Soft lavender purple wash (#9B7FC7), watercolor style. Covers roughly the back 15-20% of the cerebrum with a gently rounded rear edge.

Cerebellum

Position: Below and slightly behind the occipital lobe, at the lower-right portion of the brain.
Visual: Soft neutral tan or warm gray (#C8B8A0) watercolor wash with the characteristic fine horizontal folial pattern. Should be visually subdued so it does not compete with the four main lobes.

Brainstem

Position: Descending downward from the underside of the cerebrum, below the temporal lobe.
