```text
Type: infographic

Learning Objective: Differentiate between normal aging, mild cognitive impairment, and dementia across multiple dimensions (Bloom Level 4 - Analyze)

Bloom Taxonomy Level: Analyze (L4)
Bloom Verb: Differentiate, distinguish, compare, contrast

Purpose: Provide an interactive visual comparison showing how normal aging, MCI, and dementia differ across multiple criteria to help users understand the distinctions

Layout: Three-column comparison table with interactive elements

Column Headers:
- Normal Aging
- Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI)
- Dementia

Comparison Dimensions (rows):

1. Memory Changes
- Normal: Occasional forgetfulness, remembers later
- MCI: Frequent forgetfulness, may not remember later, but compensates
- Dementia: Severe memory problems, cannot compensate, affects daily life
- Interactive: Hover to see concrete examples for each

2. Independence
- Normal: Fully independent in all activities
- MCI: Independent, may take longer or use strategies
- Dementia: Requires assistance with complex or routine tasks
- Interactive: Click to see list of affected activities

3. Awareness of Problems
- Normal: Usually aware, may joke about "senior moments"
- MCI: Aware and often concerned
- Dementia: Often unaware or minimizes problems
- Interactive: Hover for explanation of anosognosia

4. Testing Performance
- Normal: Scores within expected range for age/education
- MCI: Scores below expected but not in dementia range
- Dementia: Scores in impaired range
- Interactive: Click to see example test score ranges

5. Rate of Progression
- Normal: Stable or very gradually changing over many years
- MCI: Noticeable change over 1-2 years
- Dementia: Progressive worsening over months to years
- Interactive: Hover to see typical progression timelines

6. Ability to Compensate
- Normal: Compensatory strategies work well (lists, calendars)
- MCI: Compensation required more often but still effective
- Dementia: Compensation insufficient for functioning
- Interactive: Click for examples of compensatory strategies

7. Impact on Work/Hobbies
- Normal: Can perform complex work, pursue hobbies
- MCI: May need extra effort but can continue
- Dementia: Cannot perform complex work, may abandon hobbies
- Interactive: Hover for specific examples

8. Social Functioning
- Normal: Normal social interactions
- MCI: Normal social functioning
- Dementia: Difficulty with complex social situations, may withdraw
- Interactive: Click to see examples of affected social situations

9. Risk of Progression
- Normal: 1-2% per year develop dementia
- MCI: 10-15% per year develop dementia
- Dementia: Progressive by definition
- Interactive: Hover to see 5-year progression statistics

10. Medical Intervention
- Normal: No specific treatment needed
- MCI: Monitoring, lifestyle interventions, treat underlying conditions
- Dementia: Medications, comprehensive care planning
- Interactive: Click to see intervention options

Interactive Features:
- Hover over any cell to highlight that row across all three columns
- Click dimension label to see detailed explanation
- Toggle "Show Examples" to display concrete scenarios for each cell
- "Compare Two" mode that highlights two columns for direct comparison
- Color coding: Green (Normal), Yellow (MCI), Orange (Dementia)
- "Print Summary" button generates printer-friendly comparison

Bottom Panel:
Key Message: "MCI represents a middle ground—more than normal aging but not yet dementia. Early recognition enables monitoring and intervention."

Visual Style: Clean table with clear visual hierarchy
Color scheme: Green → Yellow → Orange gradient showing progression
Responsive design: Table becomes accordion-style on narrow screens

Implementation: HTML/CSS/JavaScript with interactive hover and click events
Canvas size: 900x600px, fully responsive
```
