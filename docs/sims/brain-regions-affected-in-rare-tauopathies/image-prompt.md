Style: Scientific watercolor illustration with soft textures and gentle shading; suitable for a high school / adult patient-education textbook on dementia. Hand-painted watercolor feel with clear anatomical accuracy.

What to Draw

Three lateral (side) view human brain illustrations arranged horizontally across the canvas, each brain representing a different rare tauopathy. All three brains should be drawn in the same orientation (left hemisphere, lateral view) with the frontal lobe pointing LEFT and the occipital lobe pointing RIGHT. All three brains should be the same size.

Layout

Each brain occupies approximately 30% of the canvas width.
Approximately 3% horizontal gap between adjacent brains.
Brains horizontally centered vertically, leaving a modest top and bottom margin (~8%).
Leave clean white space around each brain for overlay markers.
Do NOT draw any dividing lines, frames, headings, or labels between the brains.

Anatomical Features Common to All Three

Each brain should show:

The recognizable outline of the cerebrum (left hemisphere, lateral view).
Characteristic gyri and sulci (wrinkled ridges and grooves) painted with soft watercolor texture.
A small visible portion of the cerebellum below and behind the cerebrum.
A visible brainstem descending below the cerebrum (important for the PSP brain on the right).
