## What to Draw

A horizontal process diagram showing the seven stages of beta-amyloid production
and aggregation, arranged from LEFT to RIGHT across the canvas. The scene is set
on a neuron cell membrane: the membrane should appear as a continuous horizontal
band (phospholipid bilayer) running across the bottom ~35-40% of the canvas.
Above the membrane is the **extracellular space** (clean, slightly off-white).
Below the membrane is the **inside of the neuron** (a very soft pale cream tint).

The cell membrane should be painted as two parallel rows of small round
phospholipid head-groups with faint tails between them — a classic biology
textbook bilayer, in soft muted teal/gray watercolor. The membrane should
continue uninterrupted across the ENTIRE width of the canvas, tying the seven
stages together into one continuous scene.

Above the membrane, spaced from left to right, paint the seven stages of the
beta-amyloid process. Each stage should be centered at the approximate x-coordinate
listed below, with its main molecule(s) placed ABOVE the membrane in the
extracellular space (except stages 1-3 where the APP protein crosses the membrane).

Leave approximately 8% clear margin at the top and bottom of the canvas for
overlay markers.

## The Seven Stages (left to right)

### Stage 1 — APP Embedded in the Membrane (x ≈ 10%)

A single large **Amyloid Precursor Protein (APP)** molecule shown as a tall
rounded blue blob (soft royal blue #3B6FB3) that CROSSES the cell membrane — part
of it sticks up into the extracellular space above, a narrow middle section
passes through the bilayer, and a small tail dangles into the neuron interior
below. The shape should look like a lollipop or a tall mushroom. Watercolor
shading, slightly darker blue at the edges.

### Stage 2 — Beta-Secretase Cuts APP (x ≈ 25%)

The same APP protein (blue), but now with a **beta-secretase enzyme** shown as a
soft green (#4CAF50) scissor-like or Pac-Man-shaped molecule approaching from
the side and making a first cut at the top of the APP where it meets the
extracellular space. A small visible gap or notch should appear in the APP at
the cut site. The enzyme stays clearly green so it reads as "the cutter."

### Stage 3 — Gamma-Secretase Cuts APP, Releasing Aβ42 (x ≈ 40%)

APP shown again (blue), with a second green **gamma-secretase enzyme** (slightly
darker green #2E7D32) cutting at a second site lower down, right at the
membrane. A small orange fragment (the freshly released Aβ42 peptide, warm
orange #F57C00) is shown just starting to float upward and away from the
membrane. The rest of the APP remains behind in the membrane.

### Stage 4 — Free Beta-Amyloid Monomers (x ≈ 55%)

Three or four **free Aβ42 monomers** floating in the extracellular space above
the membrane. Each monomer is a small squiggly or bean-shaped orange peptide
(#F57C00) with a soft watercolor outline. They should be separated from each
other, drifting, not yet sticking together.

### Stage 5 — Oligomer Formation (x ≈ 70%)

Two or three orange monomers now clumped together into a small cluster
(**oligomer**), with visible contact points where they touch. The color may
deepen slightly toward burnt orange (#E65100) to suggest they are becoming
toxic. Still clearly made of individual peptide units, but sticking together.

### Stage 6 — Protofibrils (x ≈ 82%)

A larger, more elongated aggregate — a **protofibril** — drawn as a short
chain or rope of many fused peptide units, red-orange in color (#D84315),
slightly irregular and thicker than the oligomer. It should look more
threadlike and denser than stage 5.

### Stage 7 — Mature Amyloid Plaque (x ≈ 95%)

A large, irregular, blob-shaped **amyloid plaque** sitting in the extracellular
space just above the membrane. Dark red / brick red watercolor (#8B2E1F) with
rough edges and internal texture suggesting tangled protein fibers. This is the
largest object in the scene — clearly the "end stage" of the process. It should
look dense, clumpy, and pathological.

## Visual Flow

- Maintain the same horizontal membrane across all seven stages so the eye
  reads the image as a single continuous story
- Each stage's main object should be roughly vertically centered in the upper
  half of the canvas so overlay markers can sit cleanly on each one
- Soft watercolor transitions between stages (subtle fading) are fine, but each
  stage should be visually distinct
- Color progression: blue (APP) → green (enzymes) → orange (monomers) →
  red-orange (oligomers/protofibrils) → dark red (mature plaque)
