# Healthy Tau vs. Abnormal Tau — Image Generation Prompt

Please generate a new image.

Critical Rule

**This image must contain absolutely no text, labels, arrows, callout lines, numbers, or
annotation marks of any kind.** All labeling will be added as an interactive HTML
overlay from a separate data file. Any embedded text will conflict with the overlay
system and must not appear.

Image Specifications

- **Format**: PNG
- **Dimensions**: 1400 × 900 px (landscape, ~3:2 ratio)
- **Background**: Clean white (#FFFFFF)
- **Style**: Scientific watercolor illustration with soft textures and gentle
  shading; suitable for a high school / adult patient-education textbook on
  dementia. Hand-painted watercolor feel with clear biological accuracy.

Overall Composition

A single image containing TWO side-by-side panels that together form one
cohesive illustration. The two panels must be clearly divided by a visible
vertical gutter running down the exact center of the canvas (at x = 700 px).
The gutter should be a soft neutral vertical band (approximately 20-30 px wide,
very pale gray or off-white) so the two panels read as a clear comparison.

- **LEFT panel** (x = 0 to ~685 px): **Healthy neuron** — intact, organized,
  vibrant. Use a soft warm glow or pale mint-green tint behind the structures
  to signal health.
- **RIGHT panel** (x = ~715 to 1400 px): **Diseased neuron** — fragmented,
  disorganized, collapsing. Use a pale warm beige or very subtle rose tint
  behind the structures to signal pathology.

Each panel must leave a 5-8% margin on its outer edges for overlay markers.
Keep the central gutter clean so the divider is obvious at a glance.

LEFT Panel — Healthy Neuron Axon Cross-Section

Draw a horizontal longitudinal section through a healthy neuron's axon. The
axon should span most of the left panel horizontally, with the cell body (soma)
partly visible at the far left edge and the axon extending toward the center
gutter.

Microtubules (BLUE)

- Three to five long, parallel horizontal "rails" running the length of the
  axon, stacked vertically with even spacing.
- Painted in soft sky blue (#6FA8DC) with a watercolor wash.
- Each rail should look smooth, continuous, and intact — no breaks or gaps.
- Suggest the subtle cylindrical tube structure of microtubules with gentle
  shading but keep the style painterly, not technical.

Normal Tau (GREEN)

- Small green "cross-tie" shapes bridging between adjacent microtubule rails at
  regular intervals (every 60-80 px along each rail).
- Shape: short rounded bars or soft dumbbell shapes that visibly attach to the
  blue microtubule rails, like railroad ties binding parallel tracks.
- Color: soft sage/leaf green (#6FBF73), watercolor style.
- They should look orderly, evenly spaced, and firmly bound to the rails.

Transport Vesicles (YELLOW)

- Three to four small round yellow vesicles (circles or ovals) sitting ON TOP
  of the microtubule rails, distributed along the axon, as if being moved along
  the rails from left to right.
- Color: warm golden yellow (#F1C232) with soft watercolor shading to suggest
  roundness.
- Optional: a very subtle directional smudge behind each vesicle to hint at
  motion along the rail.

Healthy Axon Outline

- The outer boundary of the axon is a soft, smooth, gently curving pale outline
  (very light tan or pale pink #F5E6E0) enclosing all the microtubules, tau,
  and vesicles.
- The axon should look full, healthy, and continuous from the soma on the far
  left all the way to the gutter on the right.

RIGHT Panel — Diseased Neuron (Alzheimer Pathology)

Draw a horizontal longitudinal section through a diseased neuron's axon in the
same orientation and scale as the left panel, so the viewer can compare them
directly. The cell body (soma) should be partly visible at the center-right
(just inside the gutter), and the axon should extend from the soma toward the
far right edge — roughly mirroring the left panel.

Broken Microtubules (FADED BLUE)

- Three to five microtubule rails in the same positions as the left panel, but
  now FRAGMENTED: each rail is broken into short disconnected pieces with
  visible gaps between segments.
- Color: faded, desaturated blue-gray (#9BB5C9), watercolor wash, lower
  saturation than the healthy blue.
- Some segments should tilt slightly, drift out of alignment, or appear to be
  dissolving at their edges to convey disintegration.

Hyperphosphorylated Tau (ORANGE)

- Orange tau blobs that have DETACHED from the microtubules and are floating
  loose in the cytoplasm between and around the broken rail segments.
- Some blobs should be small individual shapes; others should be clumped
  together into larger irregular aggregates.
- Color: warm orange (#E67E22), watercolor with slightly rougher edges than the
  healthy green tau.
- They must clearly NOT be bound to the microtubule rails — they float freely
  in the space between fragments.

Neurofibrillary Tangles (DARK RED / MAROON)

- One or two larger, denser, tangled knot-like structures inside the cell body
  (soma) at the right side of the panel. These are the neurofibrillary tangles.
- Shape: irregular twisted bundles of filaments, like a tangled ball of yarn.
- Color: deep maroon / dark red (#8B2C3D) with darker watercolor edges and a
  slightly fibrous texture to suggest the paired helical filaments.

Disrupted Transport Vesicles

- Three to four small yellow vesicles similar to the left panel, but now
  STRANDED: scattered off the rails, tilted at odd angles, stuck against
  broken rail segments, or trapped in clumps of orange tau.
- Same yellow color (#F1C232) but with a slightly duller, less vibrant feel.
- No directional motion hints — they should look stuck.

Diseased Axon Outline

- The outer boundary of the diseased axon should be the same pale outline
  style as the healthy one, but slightly irregular, pinched, or wavering in
  places to suggest structural stress.

Central Gutter

- A clean, obvious vertical divider running the full height of the canvas at
  the center (approximately x = 690 to x = 720 px).
- Fill with a soft neutral pale gray (#EDEDED) or very faint off-white.
- No text, no icons, no labels in the gutter.
- The gutter should visually separate the two scenes clearly so students
  immediately see this is a comparison.

Style Notes

- Soft watercolor scientific illustration style throughout, consistent between
  both panels so they feel like one artwork.
- Clean white background outside the pale panel tints.
- Gentle shading and texture; avoid hard technical outlines or CAD-like
  precision.
- Both panels must use the same scale, orientation, and level of detail so the
  comparison reads clearly.
- Leave a 5-8% margin on the outer edges of each panel for overlay markers
  that will be added later.
- Do NOT add any text, labels, arrows, numbers, leader lines, legend boxes,
  title bars, or annotations of any kind.
- Do NOT draw a whole human figure, skull, or brain — just the two neuron
  axon sections side by side.
