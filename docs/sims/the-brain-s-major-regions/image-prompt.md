Critical Rule

This image must contain absolutely no text, labels, arrows, callout lines, numbers, or annotation marks of any kind. All labeling will be added as an interactive HTML overlay from a separate data file. Any embedded text will conflict with the overlay system and must not appear.

Image Specifications

Format: PNG

Dimensions: 1200 × 900 px (landscape, 4:3 ratio)
Background: Clean white (#FFFFFF)
Style: Scientific watercolor illustration with soft textures and gentle shading; suitable for a high school / adult patient-education textbook on dementia. Hand-painted watercolor feel with clear anatomical accuracy.

What to Draw

A mid-sagittal (mid-line cut) view of the human brain, showing the inside of the brain as if sliced vertically down the center from front to back. This view reveals both the outer cerebral cortex AND the inner structures like the hippocampus.

The brain should be oriented with the frontal lobe on the LEFT side of the canvas and the occipital lobe on the RIGHT side of the canvas. The brain should fill approximately 80-85% of the canvas, horizontally centered. Leave a 5% margin on all sides for overlay markers that may sit outside the brain boundary.

The image should show:

The recognizable outline of the human cerebrum in mid-sagittal cross-section.
The cerebral cortex as the outer wrinkled layer of the cerebrum (characteristic gyri and sulci).
The four cerebral lobes distinctly separated by subtle but visible boundary lines.
The hippocampus visible as an interior curved structure deep inside the temporal region (shown because the brain is cut open).
The cerebellum at the lower back of the brain with its characteristic fine folial pattern.
A small portion of the brainstem descending below (neutral gray/tan).

Visual: Soft warm orange wash (#E8833A), watercolor style. Small curved/C-shaped structure clearly distinct from the surrounding temporal lobe tissue.

7. Cerebellum (PINK)

Visual: Soft rose pink wash (#E68FA8), watercolor style. Show the characteristic fine horizontal folial (tree-like) pattern of the cerebellum.
