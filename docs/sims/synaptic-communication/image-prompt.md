What to Draw

A detailed cross-sectional illustration of a single chemical synapse between two neurons, showing the complete anatomy needed to explain how neurons communicate. The composition is a diagonal arrangement:

Presynaptic neuron (sender) enters from the upper-left of the canvas. Show a portion of its cell body on the far upper-left edge, with a long axon extending diagonally down toward the center-right. The axon ends in a bulbous axon terminal (synaptic bouton) positioned in the upper-middle area of the canvas.
Postsynaptic neuron (receiver) is positioned in the lower-right of the canvas. Show a portion of its cell body on the far lower-right, with a dendrite extending diagonally up and to the left toward the center. The top surface of the dendrite comes very close to — but does NOT touch — the axon terminal of the first neuron.
Between the axon terminal and the dendrite, leave a clearly visible synaptic cleft (a narrow white/very pale gap of about 40-60 px) running diagonally across the middle of the canvas. This cleft is the most important visual feature.

The composition should leave roughly 5-8% margin around the edges for overlay markers.

Color Coding

Use these soft watercolor colors consistently:

Presynaptic neuron (sender): Pale blue wash (#A8C8E8) for the cell body, axon, and axon terminal. Slightly darker blue edges (#6FA8DC) for subtle definition.
Postsynaptic neuron (receiver): Pale green wash (#B8DCA8) for the cell body and dendrite. Slightly darker green edges (#7FBF7F) for subtle definition.
Synaptic vesicles: Bright blue small circles (#3A7BC8), roughly 20-30 px in diameter.
Neurotransmitter molecules: Bright orange small dots (#F39C2E), roughly 6-10 px in diameter.
Receptor sites: Purple lock-shaped structures (#6B4F9B) embedded in the postsynaptic membrane.
Electrical signal (action potential): Soft yellow glow/wave (#F7DC4E) with a gentle halo effect.
Background: Clean white (#FFFFFF).

Specific Elements to Include

1. Presynaptic Neuron (upper-left)

Part of a pale blue neuron cell body on the far upper-left corner.
A long, tapering pale blue axon extending diagonally from the cell body toward the center of the canvas.
A bulbous axon terminal (synaptic bouton) at the end of the axon, shaped like a rounded club or bulb.

2. Action Potential (electrical signal)

A soft yellow glowing wave traveling along the axon, positioned about two-thirds of the way down the axon (between the cell body and the terminal).
Shown as a gentle yellow halo or band wrapping around the axon.
Suggests motion without any arrows or text.

3. Synaptic Vesicles (inside axon terminal)

About 10 small bright blue circles floating inside the axon terminal.
Show 2-3 of them near the bottom edge of the terminal fusing with the presynaptic membrane (shown as partially merged with the membrane, opening into the cleft).
The remaining 7-8 vesicles are freely floating higher up inside the terminal.

4. Synaptic Cleft (the gap)

A clearly visible narrow gap (~40-60 px wide) between the axon terminal and the dendrite.
Runs diagonally across the middle of the canvas.
Pale white/cream with very subtle shading so the neurotransmitter dots stand out.

5. Neurotransmitter Molecules (in the cleft)

About 30 small bright orange dots scattered throughout the synaptic cleft.
Show them in motion: some near the presynaptic membrane (just released from fusing vesicles), some mid-cleft, and some near the postsynaptic receptors (about to bind).
Random scatter pattern, not in a grid.

6. Receptor Sites (on postsynaptic membrane)

About 6-8 small purple lock-shaped structures embedded in the top surface of the dendrite, facing the synaptic cleft.
Each receptor should have a small keyhole-like notch facing upward toward the cleft.
A few should already have an orange neurotransmitter dot sitting in the keyhole (bound).

7. Postsynaptic Neuron (lower-right)

A pale green dendrite extending from the lower-right up toward the center.
The dendrite surface facing the cleft holds the receptor sites.
Part of the postsynaptic cell body visible in the far lower-right corner.
